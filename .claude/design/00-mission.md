# 00 — Mission

## Who this is for

A person in Portugal who has a fiscal life they did not choose to have: faturas to classify once a
year, a landlord's recibos, an IRS deadline, a dívida they are not sure exists. They are not an
accountant. They are not stupid. They have been made to feel stupid by state software.

They are not "users of a platform". They are someone doing a chore, on a laptop, probably in the
evening, probably slightly anxious about getting it wrong.

## What Fatura Boa is

A tool that reads what the State already knows about you, in the session you already have open, and
tells you plainly what it says and what you might do about it.

That is the whole product. It is not a platform, not an assistant, not a dashboard.

## What it must transmit

**Competence without authority.** It should feel like it was built by someone who read the actual
law, and who is not pretending to be the government.

**Calm.** The subject is money and deadlines. The interface must lower the temperature, never raise
it. Nothing blinks, nothing counts down, nothing is urgent unless it genuinely is.

**Legibility as respect.** Every figure has a source. Every claim has a test. Every number is an
indicator to confirm, and says so. Treating someone as capable of checking your work is the most
respectful thing an interface can do.

**Being checkable.** Not "trust us". *Here is the hash, here is the source, here is the article
number.* The verifiability is not a feature bolted on the side; it is the reason the thing is
allowed to exist.

## What it must never transmit

**Authority it does not have.** No crests, no seals, no `.gov` mimicry, no "official". The palette
is institutional on purpose and the page says plainly it is not a state service. Never blur that
line to seem more credible.

**Urgency as a lever.** Deadlines are shown because they are real, in the same visual weight as
everything else. Never as a red badge designed to make someone act faster than they can think.

**Certainty it does not have.** Where the data is unvalidated, it says so, in the interface, next
to the number. "Em desenvolvimento — confirma" is a design element, not an apology.

**That it is doing something on your behalf.** It reads. You act. The moment the interface suggests
otherwise it has misrepresented the risk the user is taking.

**Salesmanship.** There is nothing to sell. No conversion funnel, no growth surface, no social
proof. The one support strip is honest about being a referral and sits at the bottom of a result,
not in the way.

## The tone

Direct, plain European Portuguese, second person singular. Short sentences. Technical words when
they are the right words, explained once. Never chirpy, never corporate, never apologetic.

Say "não sabemos" when it is true. Say "isto pode estar errado, confirma" when it is true. The
credibility comes from the places where it admits limits, not from the places where it sounds
confident.

## The one-line test

> Would someone who understands tax law read this page and think it was made by someone who also
> understands tax law — and would someone who understands none of it still know what to do next?

If either half fails, the design has failed, however it looks.
